# Left-hand-tap notation — the final adversarial pass

**Status: ANALYSIS COMPLETE 2026-08-11, AWAITING SIGNATURES (S1–S6 below).** The user asked for
one deliberate final pass on whether left-hand taps should get a notation distinct from
hammer-ons — "that is the one real decision that created this condition" — with the complete
picture from the ground-up simplicity analysis in view. Ten agents ran it: three record/
evidence passes (every prior ruling verbatim, the display surfaces as shipped, GP-format and
corpus measurements), four advocates (for the split, for merged, a decomposition of the
decision itself, a gameplay-surface specialist), two adversarial cross-examiners, and a judge
under the kill-named-alternatives-one-by-one discipline.

## The causal claim, corrected

The user's premise was half right, and the half decides the question. There WAS exactly one
real founding decision and it DID create the machinery condition — but the causal ingredient
was the **storage merge, not the notation merge**. The founding sentence was notation language
("the hammer-on states accurately — no separate notation"); its unexamined implementation
shadow was `left_hand_tapped → Hammer` — storing the derivable datum (direction, which goes
stale when a neighbour changes, forcing the repair engine) while discarding the authored datum
(provenance, whose absence makes re-derivation dangerous, forcing the windows, settle-on-undo,
D14, and the stale-flag kill class).

Proof by counterfactual, both directions: hold the mark merged and split storage
(DERIVED-DIRECTION) → the repair engine, recalc window, D14, and stale-flag class all die.
Hold storage merged and split the mark → every mechanism survives untouched, because the
windows protect stored bytes, not pixels — and the mark is not even drawable (no datum exists
to drive a truthful tap glyph). **Not one line of machinery traces to the drawn mark.** The
record's real defect was a category slip: the earlier rejections priced NOTATION costs
(two-T collision, unknown 3D cell) against what was actually a STORAGE benefit (lifecycle), so
the true cause survived three reviews inside a display question's costume.

## The answer

**No alternate notation for left-hand taps — not in 2D, not in 3D, not editor-only, under any
storage design.** Third rejection, and unlike the prior two it is on terminal grounds:

- Under merged storage (RADICAL-MIN / MINIMAL-EXPRESSION) a truthful tap notation is
  **undrawable** — no stored datum exists; derivation failure is the only signal, and it is
  alias-blind and conflates deliberate taps with breakage.
- Under split storage (DERIVED-DIRECTION) it is **player-useless** — detection is one lenient
  row (a tap from silence and a hammer onto a damped string are the identical signal), scoring
  treats attack kind as cosmetic, the motor act is one family, and the WoR mark-what-changes-
  the-action principle terminates at the pick/no-pick boundary the surfaces already draw.
- The alias zone — the only place a tap mark would carry unique information — is exactly where
  it would contradict the drawn geometry (a "from nowhere" glyph beside a visible ascending
  predecessor), and D13 guarantees the merged instruction already works there.

What is drawn, driven by what: the merged mark draws the RESOLVED attack — one reading on
every surface including the game. The hollow cue (DERIVED-DIRECTION only) draws DERIVATION
STATE — editor chrome on both editor surfaces, never in the game. Stored intent drives no
permanent ink anywhere.

Named alternatives, each killed individually: the lettered T-plate (plates are the picking
hand's signature; `T` is the right-hand tap's); the per-pair 2D connector (user-rejected,
and now dataless in every survivor); the distinct 3D gem / seventeenth atlas cell (a mark
corresponding to nothing the player does or the game judges — a lying gem even if the cell
were free, and it is not: fifth atlas row, full rebake); the 3D tint shortcut (killed on fact:
255-core signature, plan-54 color ownership, the 55-Q1 supersession); and the genuinely
unpriced per-note intent modifier (tap dot / circled-T class) — now priced and killed on
merits, with its strongest surviving form recorded on the shelf (S5).

## Effect on the storage decision

The notation pass **strengthens DERIVED-DIRECTION**, three ways: (a) reversibility — only it
keeps the notation question cheap to reopen forever (one atlas edit if felt confusion ever
materializes), while the merged-storage designs foreclose it permanently by destroying the
datum at import; (b) a new, independent import-fidelity argument — the ≤310 ascending-
predecessor corpus taps (upper bound) are GP-authored intent that merged storage silently
destroys and split storage stores verbatim, a data-loss argument fully separate from the
lifecycle argument that won the ground-up judgment (the corpus holds 1,783 played left-hand-
tap instances in all; the GP format's own data model IS the split model, and `LeftHandTapped`
never co-occurs with a hopo flag — a structural zero); (c) it closes the winner's own display
residues (the invisible LeftTap/resolved-Hammer fork is met by sanctioned opt-in chrome).
